## What the IP Box regime is
<!-- facts: MB-F20 -->
The Cyprus IP Box provides an 80% exemption for qualifying net profit from qualifying intangible assets. The amount of net profit that qualifies is calculated under the nexus approach; the exemption is not a flat deduction from all company profit. The Tax Department describes relevant income as including royalties, embedded income and other qualifying income derived from qualifying intangible assets.

Cyprus's headline corporate tax rate is 15% from 2026. As simple, non-personalized headline arithmetic only, applying 15% to the 20% left taxable after an 80% exemption gives 3% on the qualifying net profit before any case-specific adjustments. This is not a promised or personalized effective rate, and it says nothing about the rate on a company's total profit: the nexus calculation, the share of total profit that qualifies and the company's facts all matter.

**Nexus-calculated** means the qualifying net profit is determined under the regime's calculation, which links the benefit to qualifying research and development activity and expenditure. The rest of the company's profit does not receive this IP Box exemption and is subject to the ordinary tax rules.

## What makes an asset and a profit qualify
<!-- facts: MB-F20, MB-F21 -->
These are useful questions for a specialist review.

**What rights does the company hold in the asset?** The ownership and licensing arrangements, including any rights retained by a founder or another company, need to be checked. A short description is not enough to establish that the Cyprus company can claim the regime.

**What research and development activity and expenditure relate to it?** The nexus calculation links the qualifying share to qualifying development expenditure. The nature, location, funding and outsourcing arrangements may affect the calculation and need to be reviewed against the applicable rules.

**What income comes from the asset?** Royalty, embedded and other income may be relevant, but each income stream and the applicable qualifying-asset rules require review.

So "I have a mobile app" is the start of the analysis, not the answer to it. The same app can qualify under one structure and not under another, depending on who owns it and who built it. The same logic separates licence and subscription income earned from the asset from ordinary service income billed by the hour.

## Why IP Box is never automatic
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F21 -->
IP Box is not automatic for every company. A Cyprus company that writes software does not receive the rate by default.

Qualification depends on the asset, the rights held, the income derived from it and the nexus calculation of qualifying net profit. That requires a technical review of the product, ownership and licensing arrangements, income and development records; it cannot be confirmed from a description in a chat.

So nobody here will tell you that you qualify, and nobody will promise you a rate. What we can say is whether your situation looks worth a proper review, and what evidence that review would ask you to produce.

If the review goes against you, nothing is broken. The company pays the normal corporate rate like any other company, which is also why IP Box should never be the only reason a structure exists.

## What a specialist would review
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F20 -->
These details help a specialist assess whether a review is worth running.

What is the product? Software, a platform, a registered or patented asset, or something closer to content and branding.

What rights exist today? You personally, a Cyprus company, an existing company elsewhere, or an arrangement that has not been formalised yet.

What research and development was done, by whom, where and under what funding or outsourcing arrangement?

How is the revenue earned? Licences, subscriptions, one off sales, or hourly services, because service income and intellectual property income are not the same thing under the regime.

These details can support a specialist review of whether any net profit falls within the regime and what evidence is needed. They do not establish eligibility or a tax result by themselves.
