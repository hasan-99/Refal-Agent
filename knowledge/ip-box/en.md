## What the IP Box regime is
<!-- facts: MB-F20 -->
The IP Box is an approved tax treatment in Cyprus that applies to profits earned from qualifying intellectual property assets. Software development is one of the main activities it was designed around.

Where it applies, it can bring the effective rate on those qualifying profits down to roughly 2.5% to 3%. Set that beside the base corporate rate of 15% from 2026 and the size of the gap explains why the regime gets so much attention.

Two words in that sentence carry all the weight. **Effective** means the result after the regime's own calculation has been run, not a separate headline rate you simply elect into. **Qualifying** means only the share of profit that the rules recognise as coming from the intellectual property itself. The rest of the company's profit stays on the normal corporate rate.

## What makes an asset and a profit qualify
<!-- facts: MB-F20, MB-F21 -->
Three questions decide most cases.

**Does the company own the asset?** The regime looks at intellectual property sitting inside the Cyprus company, not at software the founder owns personally and lets the company use.

**Did the company genuinely develop it?** The regime rewards real development activity. In house work, meaning your own team or development that the company directs and pays for, is what builds the case. Buying a finished product and parking it in Cyprus is a different situation entirely.

**Where was the development done, and by whom?** The qualifying share of the profit is linked to the development effort and spend the company itself carried. Development performed entirely outside the company's own activity reduces that share.

So "I have a mobile app" is the start of the analysis, not the answer to it. The same app can qualify under one structure and not under another, depending on who owns it and who built it. The same logic separates licence and subscription income earned from the asset from ordinary service income billed by the hour.

## Why IP Box is never automatic
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F21 -->
IP Box is not automatic for every company. A Cyprus company that writes software does not receive the rate by default.

Qualification depends on the nature of the intellectual property itself and on whether it is genuinely developed in house. That is a technical review of your product, your ownership chain and your development records, and it is not something anyone can confirm from a description in a chat.

So nobody here will tell you that you qualify, and nobody will promise you a rate. What we can say is whether your situation looks worth a proper review, and what evidence that review would ask you to produce.

If the review goes against you, nothing is broken. The company pays the normal corporate rate like any other company, which is also why IP Box should never be the only reason a structure exists.

## What we look at before anything is claimed
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F20 -->
Four answers tell us whether a review is worth running.

What is the product? Software, a platform, a registered or patented asset, or something closer to content and branding.

Who owns it today? You personally, an existing company somewhere else, or nothing formalised yet.

Who built it, and where? An in house team, contractors, a partner, or a mix of all three.

How is the revenue earned? Licences, subscriptions, one off sales, or hourly services, because service income and intellectual property income are not the same thing under the regime.

With those, a specialist can say what share of your profit could realistically fall in scope, and what the regime would expect you to prove.
