## What is on the public company file
<!-- facts: MB-F16 -->
A Cyprus company is a registered legal entity, so part of its file is public by design.

The Registrar of Companies holds the company name and number, the registered address, the Memorandum and Articles of Association, the directors, the secretary and the registered shareholders. That file is searchable, and a supplier, a counterparty or a competitor can look at it.

What is not on that open file is the beneficial ownership information collected under the anti money laundering rules. That sits in a separate UBO register, and access to it is restricted to authorities and to regulated entities such as banks and licensed service providers rather than open to the general public. The scope of that access has changed more than once across the EU, so the current position is confirmed before anyone relies on it.

The honest summary is this. Your name as a registered shareholder or director is visible. Your identity as beneficial owner is recorded, access controlled, and not advertised.

## Legitimate structural privacy, and what it can do
<!-- facts: MB-F16 -->
Structural privacy is normal and lawful. Ordinary companies use it every day, and it is mostly about who sees what in the commercial world, not about hiding from the state.

Common, legitimate arrangements:

- A holding company is the registered shareholder of the operating company, so the public file of the trading entity shows a legal entity rather than a list of individuals.
- A nominee shareholder or nominee director appears on the public register, while a declaration of trust records the real owner, who is disclosed in full to the UBO register, to the bank and to the service provider.
- The registered address receives official correspondence, so a private home address does not circulate.
- Confidentiality clauses in shareholder agreements and service contracts limit what partners and providers may disclose.
- Personal data is processed under GDPR, which gives the individual rights over how it is handled.

All of these reduce casual public visibility. None of them removes the company's duty to tell the authorities and the bank who is behind it.

## What a nominee actually is
<!-- kind: example; facts: MB-F16 -->
Nominee services are one of the most misread items in company structuring, so precision helps.

A nominee shareholder holds the shares in their own name on the public register, under a signed declaration of trust stating that the shares belong to you. A nominee director sits on the board for administrative purposes. In both cases the real owner is documented, and that documentation goes to the beneficial ownership register, to the bank during onboarding, and to the licensed provider who operates the arrangement.

What a nominee is not: it is not anonymity, it is not a shield from a tax authority, and it is not a way to tell a bank that somebody else owns the money. A nominee changes who appears on the public page. It does not change who the owner is in law or in the compliance file.

There is also a practical cost. A nominee structure adds an annual service relationship and extra paperwork, and some banks and payment providers ask more questions when they see one. It earns its place when there is a real commercial reason, not as a default setting.

## Where the line is drawn
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F16 -->
There is a clear difference between arranging privacy and concealing the Ultimate Beneficial Owner.

Privacy means the public page shows less, while the regulator, the bank and the UBO register see everything. That is lawful and it is supported.

Concealment means building a structure whose purpose is to stop an authority, a bank or a tax administration from identifying the real owner, usually to avoid costs, taxes or controls. That is not supported, and it is not something we help design, document or operate. A request to be invisible to every government authority cannot be met, in Cyprus or anywhere in the EU, because identifying the beneficial owner is a legal duty of the company, of the bank and of every licensed service provider involved.

The lawful alternative is real, and it usually delivers most of what was actually wanted. Keep personal details off the open commercial file through a holding entity, a registered address and a properly documented nominee arrangement, then disclose in full through the regulated channel. If the goal is a lower tax burden rather than invisibility, that is a different question with legitimate answers, and it belongs with a tax specialist who can look at residency, structure and treaties.

## The bank and the regulator always identify the owner
<!-- facts: MB-F16 -->
Every bank, payment institution and licensed corporate service provider runs KYC and AML checks before an account or a service goes live. Identifying the Ultimate Beneficial Owner is a core part of those checks and is not optional for them.

In practice the file records who owns and who controls the company, where the funds behind the activity come from, and in some cases how the wider wealth was built. These are standard compliance steps applied to everyone, not suspicion about a particular client.

The consequence matters for planning. A structure designed to keep the owner out of sight tends to fail at exactly the point the client cares about, which is the account opening or the payment gateway. Files that look opaque attract more questions, take longer, and are declined more often. A clean, fully disclosed ownership chain is the fastest route to a working account, which is why the structure is built that way from the start.

## What we need to know to answer this concretely
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F16 -->
Privacy questions have very different answers depending on what is actually being protected, so three short answers make the conversation specific.

What do you want to keep private, and from whom? A home address away from suppliers, a name away from competitors, and a name away from a tax authority are three separate questions with three separate answers.

Who else will be in the structure? Partners, an investor arriving later, or a group company abroad all change what the public file will show.

Where are you tax resident, and where will the banking sit? Disclosure duties follow the bank and your own residency as much as they follow Cyprus.

With those answers, a specialist can say precisely which details stay off the open file and which ones must be disclosed through the regulated channel.
