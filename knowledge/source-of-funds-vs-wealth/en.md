## Two different questions, usually asked together
<!-- facts: MB-F35, MB-F36 -->
Compliance files use two terms that sound similar and are not.

**Source of Funds** is the narrow one. It asks where the specific money for this specific transaction came from, and by what route it reached the account it sits in today.

**Source of Wealth** is the wider one. It asks how your wealth was built up over the years in the first place.

They are collected separately because a file can be strong on one and thin on the other. A transfer from a named personal account is a clear Source of Funds, and the reviewer may still want to understand how that account came to hold the amount. Answering both early is usually faster than answering one and being asked again.

## Source of Funds: the path of the money in this deal
<!-- facts: MB-F35 -->
Source of Funds follows one amount from its origin to the account that will pay for the transaction.

Two common shapes:

**Proceeds of a property sale.** The money is what came out of a specific property you sold. The file normally shows the sale contract or deed, evidence that ownership transferred, and the bank credit where the proceeds landed.

**A transfer from a personal bank account.** The money has been held in an account in your own name. The file normally shows the identity of the account holder, account history covering the amount, and the transfer instruction into the transaction.

Company profits follow the same logic. If the money is a dividend or a distribution from a company you own, the chain runs from the company accounts, to the decision to distribute, to the payment into your personal account, to the payment into the deal. It is accepted when the chain is complete and documented, not because of the label on it.

The principle is continuity. Every step between the origin and the payment should be visible, with nothing unexplained in the middle.

## Source of Wealth: how the wealth was built over the years
<!-- facts: MB-F36 -->
Source of Wealth is a history rather than a single transfer. It describes how the overall wealth was accumulated, and the usual building blocks are business profits, inheritance, employment income, and earlier investments.

**Business profits.** Your ownership of the company, how long it has traded, and financial statements or tax returns covering the relevant years.

**Inheritance.** The will or succession document, the estate paperwork, and evidence that the assets were actually received.

**Earlier investments.** The purchase and the disposal of the asset, and what happened to the proceeds afterwards.

Not every file needs the full history. Source of Funds is the standard requirement for the deal itself. The wider Source of Wealth is asked for in some cases, typically when the amount is large relative to the profile, or when the money has moved through several steps before arriving.

## Why it is asked, and the limits of what can be said
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F37 -->
Banks and immigration authorities require documentation of the Source of Funds for the deal, and in some cases the general Source of Wealth as well. It is a standard compliance step under KYC and AML rules, applied to every file of this type. It is not a reaction to anything about you personally.

Three limits are worth knowing from the start.

The requirement belongs to them, not to us. We can prepare the file properly and explain what is normally asked, and we cannot waive a document that an institution or an authority decides it needs.

The review timetable belongs to them too. How long an assessment takes depends on the institution, on how complete the pack is, and on the case itself, so no timeline is promised from our side.

Acceptance is their decision. A complete and consistent file is what the process rewards, and nobody can guarantee an outcome on behalf of a bank or an authority.

## What is never requested inside a chat
<!-- kind: boundary -->
There is a hard privacy rule around this topic.

REFAL never asks for a password, for card data, or for a sensitive bank statement inside a chat conversation. If anything in a chat appears to ask for those, that is not how this process works.

What a chat is for is scope: which category of evidence applies to your case, roughly what the pack will contain, and what the next step is.

The documents themselves go through the proper channel with the specialist handling your file, where they are received and stored under the firm's own handling rules. So you can describe the situation here in general terms, say that the money comes from a property sale or from an inheritance, and keep the actual statements and certificates for that channel.

## What we need to know to be specific
<!-- kind: discovery -->
A few short answers turn a general explanation into the actual list for your file.

Where is the money sitting right now? A personal account, a company account, the proceeds of a sale, or an inheritance.

Whose name is on the account the payment leaves from? Yours, a company you own, or a third party. A third party payment changes the documentation entirely.

How many steps sit between the origin and the payment? One transfer is simple. Four transfers across two countries needs more paperwork.

Which country holds the funds today, and in what currency?

With those answers, the specialist can tell you which documents apply to your case, what order to collect them in, and where delays usually come from.
