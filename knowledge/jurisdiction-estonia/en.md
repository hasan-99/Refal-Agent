## What Estonian e-Residency is, and what it is not
<!-- facts: MB-J2 -->
Estonia is strong on digital solutions, and that part is not in dispute. e-Residency is a state issued digital identity that lets a non resident register and administer an Estonian company online, sign documents electronically and deal with the administration remotely. For a founder whose priority is running the company from a screen, that is a real advantage.

What it is not is residence. e-Residency gives a digital identity, not the right to live anywhere, and nothing about property stability or a family settlement comes with it.

Corporate tax in Estonia attaches at the point profit is distributed rather than while profit sits inside the company. That suits a founder who reinvests everything, and it changes the picture for one who wants to take money out on a regular basis.

Estonia and Cyprus are both European Union member states, so both give you an EU company and an EU VAT registration where the activity requires one.

## Where the Cyprus environment is structurally different
<!-- facts: MB-J2 -->
Cyprus joins up three things that are usually separate: the company, the residency and the property.

**Real presence.** Offices, staff, a local address and management on the ground. Substance matters the moment a bank, a payment provider or a tax authority asks where the company is actually run from.

**Residency routes.** Living in Cyprus is a documented path for the founder and the family, not a side effect of a company registration.

**Property.** The same capital can sit in a tangible asset instead of existing only as a line on a balance sheet.

**Intellectual property.** Cyprus has an IP Box regime for qualifying intellectual property, which is why software and SaaS files are usually read from the IP angle first.

That is a structural difference, and a difference is not a ranking. The combination matters a great deal to someone who intends to move, and matters much less to someone who will never set foot in either country.

## What a country comparison cannot settle
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-J0 -->
We do not run other countries down and we do not claim to be the right answer every time. Estonia is a well built system, and for some businesses it is the better fit.

The right jurisdiction depends on the specific business. Where your customers are, where the money is received, what the activity really is, whether you need to live somewhere, and what you already own elsewhere all move the answer. A comparison made without those answers is not a real comparison.

There are also things we will not state. We do not publish another country's tax rates, incorporation times or accounting fees as though they were ours to stand behind; those belong to a specialist in that country. Whether a bank accepts an account is decided by the institution, in Cyprus and in Estonia alike. And none of this is personal tax advice, because your own tax residency can override the whole structure.

## What we need to know to compare properly
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-J2 -->
Four answers turn country labels into a real comparison.

Where are your customers and where is the money received? Inside the EU, outside it, or both.

Do you need to actually live somewhere? If residency for you or the family is part of the plan, the field narrows quickly.

What is the activity? Software and intellectual property, trading, services, or holding.

What happens to the profit? Reinvested in full, or distributed to shareholders on a regular basis. That single answer changes how a system that taxes at distribution compares with a system that taxes profit at company level.

With those four, the discussion moves from countries to your file, and the tax detail goes to a tax specialist rather than to a general description.
