## Completed property and who it suits
<!-- facts: MB-F46 -->
A completed property is finished and available now. You can walk through the actual unit, see the real finish, the real light and the real surroundings, and then move in or let it without waiting.

That makes it the fit for two kinds of buyer. The first wants a home to use now, whether the driver is relocation, schools or simply a short horizon. The second wants rental income that starts quickly, because a unit that is already finished can be let as soon as it is furnished and connected.

The trade offs are real as well. Payment is concentrated. A completed unit is normally settled over a much shorter window than a build, so more of the capital has to be available early. Choice is also limited to what exists today, so the specific floor, orientation or layout you had in mind may simply not be in that project any more. And the specification is whatever was built, not whatever you would have chosen.

## Off-plan property and who it suits
<!-- facts: MB-F47 -->
An off-plan property is bought before it is finished, from the developer, on the basis of the plans, the written specification and the delivery date set in the contract.

It suits the buyer who needs an easier payment plan and can wait. Payments are staged across the construction period and usually tied to building milestones, so the capital goes in gradually instead of in one block. It also suits the buyer who wants first choice, because early in a project the full range of units is still open.

The trade offs sit on the other side. You are buying from drawings, so the specification attached to the contract is the protection, not the brochure. The delivery date depends on the construction schedule and on the developer. There is no use and no income until handover. And the contract terms on delay, on the construction warranty and on what happens to money already paid are the terms to read closely, because on an off-plan purchase they are the whole of your protection.

## The same four questions separate them
<!-- facts: MB-F46, MB-F47 -->
**When do you need it?** Completed delivers now. Off-plan delivers on the date written into the contract, and that date belongs to the build.

**How does the money flow?** Completed concentrates the payment early. Off-plan spreads it across the construction period, which is the main reason buyers choose it.

**What exactly are you buying?** Completed is the unit you can inspect before you sign. Off-plan is the unit described in the contract and in the specification attached to it.

**When does it start being used?** A finished unit can be lived in or let once it is ready. An off-plan unit does neither until handover.

One term worth knowing: a first sale from the developer means the property has not been owned before, which is what places it in the new property category for VAT purposes. A resale between owners is a different transaction, and which category a specific property falls into is checked for that property.

## What this comparison does not settle
<!-- kind: boundary -->
The comparison tells you which type suits a situation. It does not make the purchase decision.

It does not say which is cheaper. Prices, availability and what is actually on offer in each project come from the live inventory at the moment you ask. A completed unit is not automatically more expensive than a comparable one under construction, and the opposite is not automatic either.

It does not forecast. Nobody can guarantee what a property will be worth later, and no predicted return or price direction is attached to either option. What can be assessed is the quality of the fundamentals: the location, the record of the developer, the specification, and the demand for that kind of unit.

It does not decide a residency application. Whether a specific purchase meets the criteria of a residency route is a separate assessment against that route's own rules, not a consequence of choosing off-plan or completed.

It does not replace the contract. Delivery timing, construction warranty and the remedies for delay are contractual terms of the specific project and are read from that contract.

## What we need to compare real options
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F46, MB-F47 -->
Three answers usually settle which side you are on.

When do you need the keys? A date you have to meet rules out anything that delivers later, and that one answer often ends the discussion.

How is the money available? All of it now, or gradually over the next period? A staged plan is the single strongest argument for a build, and ready capital is the strongest argument for a finished unit.

What is the property for? Living in it, letting it, or holding it as part of a wider plan. The purpose decides which trade offs matter and which are irrelevant to you.

With those three, the matching options can be pulled from the live inventory and compared on real figures instead of in the abstract.
