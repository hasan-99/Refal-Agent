## The two rates on new property
<!-- facts: MB-F48 -->
There are two VAT rates in play on new property in Cyprus.

The base rate is **19%**. That is the standard rate applied to a new property.

A reduced rate of **5%** may apply to a qualifying purchase or construction of a primary residence, subject to statutory conditions and residence-use obligations. It is not a general discount or automatic; eligibility is assessed for the specific buyer and property.

So the correct way to read the two numbers is this: 19% is the starting point, and 5% is a conditional outcome that has to be established rather than assumed.

## What the reduced rate actually depends on
<!-- facts: MB-F48 -->
The reduced rate depends on the statutory conditions for a qualifying primary residence, including the buyer's eligibility and the required use of the residence.

That is why the questions concern the buyer and intended use as well as the property. The law includes conditions for purchase or construction of a qualifying primary residence and obligations on residence use after the transaction. A specialist must verify the conditions and continuing obligations for the case.

Whether a company purchase, letting plan or other arrangement meets the statutory conditions cannot be decided from a generic description. Have the specific buyer, property and intended use reviewed before relying on the reduced rate.

The relief is applied for and assessed. Until that assessment is done on a specific file, the accurate statement is that the reduced rate may apply, not that it will.

## Why no VAT figure is given before the check
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F49 -->
VAT on a purchase is calculated precisely by the team, for the specific property and the specific buyer. No figure is quoted before that calculation.

It depends on whether the property is treated as new, on how it will be used, on who the buyer is, and on whether the conditions for the reduced rate are met in that particular case. Those inputs sit in the file, not in a general rule.

A loose generic number, a rounded example or an invented calculation would look helpful and would be wrong often enough to matter. A buyer who plans around an approximate figure and then meets the real one has been badly served, and on a property purchase the gap is not small.

The same caution applies at the edges of the question. Whether a plot of land, a sale between private owners, or a mixed use property falls inside or outside VAT is determined for that property, and it is confirmed rather than assumed in advance.

## What sits next to VAT and is not VAT
<!-- facts: MB-F48 -->
Three things get grouped with VAT and are separate from it.

**Transfer fees and government charges.** These are their own charges with their own basis. They are confirmed against the official schedule current at the time of the purchase rather than quoted from memory, and they are presented separately from the VAT figure so the total is readable.

**Resale property.** The 19% and 5% rates described here attach to new property. A sale between private owners is a different transaction with a different treatment, and whether a specific property counts as new is checked for that property.

**The residency investment threshold.** The €300,000 figure that appears in the permanent residency routes is a criterion of the residency rules, not a VAT rule. How the two interact on a specific purchase is confirmed on that file before it is relied on.

## What the team needs to calculate your VAT
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F49 -->
Four answers let the VAT be calculated exactly instead of approximately.

Which property? The specific unit, its project, and whether it is a first sale from the developer.

Who is buying? An individual or a company, and whether the buyer will live in the property themselves.

What is the use? Living in it personally, letting it, or holding it inside a wider structure.

What else is in the plan? A residency application, financing, or a possible later resale all touch the VAT position and are better known at the start.

With those, the figure comes back as a calculation on your file, with the reasoning behind it, rather than as a number that has to be revised later.
