## Limassol, the international business city
<!-- facts: MB-F51 -->
Limassol (Lemesos, Λεμεσός, ليماسول) is the international one. Coastal, a business hub, and the luxury end of the market.

**Price band.** The most expensive of the four, with the highest priced stock.

**Who buys here.** Files built around capital growth and luxury investment. High net worth investors, and international corporate activity: companies placing an office, moving executives, or putting staff in long stay accommodation.

**Character.** The concentration of international business is the reason the city reads the way it does. Shipping, services, technology and professional firms sit here, so demand for quality housing comes from companies as well as from private buyers.

If the file combines a corporate presence in Cyprus with a home that has to hold its standing, Limassol is the profile that matches. If the priority is the lowest entry price, it is not.

## Larnaca, growth and the airport
<!-- facts: MB-F52 -->
Larnaca (Larnaka, Λάρνακα, لارنكا) is the fast growth one. Coastal, and the closest of the four cities to the main international airport.

**Price band.** Medium to rising. It sits below Limassol and has been moving upward rather than sitting still.

**Who buys here.** Investors looking for growth opportunities and for rental income. Rental yields are a strong part of the case, and the city carries major infrastructure development plans that are the reason the growth story exists at all.

**Character.** Airport proximity matters more than people expect. It shapes short stay rental demand, it suits owners who fly in and out, and it is the practical answer for anyone who will be travelling often.

Larnaca is the city most often compared against Limassol. The short version of that comparison: Limassol is the established premium market, Larnaca is the growth market at a lower entry level.

## Paphos, lifestyle and tourism
<!-- facts: MB-F53 -->
Paphos (Pafos, Πάφος, بافوس) is the lifestyle one. Tourism driven, and noticeably quieter than Limassol.

**Price band.** Medium.

**Who buys here.** International buyers, holiday homes, and owners who let the property out on both short term and long term arrangements. It is also a common choice for family living, because the pace is slower and the environment is calmer than in the business cities.

**Character.** A strong seasonal visitor economy sits next to a settled international resident community. That combination is what gives the city its mix of holiday use and permanent living, and it is why the same apartment can serve a family in winter and a visitor in summer.

For a buyer whose first sentence is about quality of life rather than about yield or corporate presence, Paphos is usually the city that profile points to.

## Nicosia, the capital and the stable rental city
<!-- facts: MB-F54 -->
Nicosia (Lefkosia, Λευκωσία, نيقوسيا) is the capital. It is the administrative, governmental and university centre of the country, and it is inland, not a seafront city.

**Price band.** Stable, and built on local demand rather than on foreign buying waves. That is why it moves less in both directions than the coastal markets.

**Who buys here.** Owners who want very stable long term rentals. The tenant base is students, corporate staff and institutional employees, which is a renewing population rather than a seasonal one.

**Character.** Ministries, the central institutions, the main universities and the head offices of domestic companies are all here. The rental demand follows the academic year and the employment cycle, not the tourist season.

If the question is where business and government sit, the answer splits: Limassol for international business, Nicosia for administration, government and the domestic corporate head office layer.

## What return a property will make, and whether prices will rise
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F55 -->
No return figure and no price forecast appears anywhere in this document, and that is deliberate.

What a property actually returns depends on the specific project, the purchase price, the expected rent and the running costs. Those inputs belong to the unit, not to the city, so a city level return number would be invented rather than calculated.

Nobody can guarantee what a property will be worth in the future. Any confident sounding projection is a guess wearing a suit, and the moment it is written down it becomes a promise we never made.

The honest criterion is the one the work is actually done against: a property with strong fundamentals in a location that is in demand. That is what survives a market that does not cooperate.

With a budget and a preferred area, options are put together and the figures are worked out on the real numbers of real units. Until then, the city profiles above describe character and relative price level, and nothing more than that.

## What this comparison does not cover
<!-- kind: boundary -->
It covers four cities: Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos and Nicosia. Resort areas such as Ayia Napa, Protaras and the smaller coastal towns are not part of this comparison. They behave as individual markets rather than as city markets, so they are assessed property by property with the team instead of through a city profile.

It does not carry unit prices or availability. Stock changes, pricing changes, and a price written into a document is out of date the week after it is written. Live figures are read from the property database at the moment you ask.

It does not rank the cities. There is no single best city, only a best fit for a stated purpose, budget and timeline.

## What turns four cities into one shortlist
<!-- kind: discovery -->
Four short answers do most of the work.

**What is the property for?** A home you will live in, an income property, a capital growth position, or a holiday base you also rent out.

**What is the budget range?** It immediately removes cities rather than ranking them.

**Who is moving with you?** If children are part of the plan, their ages decide which school options are realistic, and international schools following British and English language curricula are part of that checklist. The city shortlist is usually built around the school before anything else.

**What has to be close?** The airport, a specific office, a university, or the sea. Proximity requirements decide more files than price bands do.

With those four, the comparison stops being general and starts being about one or two cities with actual units behind them.
