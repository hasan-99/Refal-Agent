## What Non Dom status actually is
<!-- facts: MB-F34 -->
Non Dom is short for non domiciled. It is a tax status for individuals, not for companies, and it sits on top of Cyprus tax residency rather than replacing it.

Two separate things have to be true before it means anything.

The first is that you become a Cyprus tax resident. That is about where you actually are, and it is tested by rules on the days you spend in Cyprus during the year.

The second is that you are treated as not domiciled in Cyprus. Domicile is about origin and long term attachment, not about this year's movements, so it is assessed on your history rather than on your calendar.

Someone born in Cyprus who has always lived there is tax resident but is normally domiciled, so the regime is not aimed at them. It is aimed at individuals who newly become Cyprus tax residents after building their lives elsewhere.

## SDC treatment and deemed-domicile rules
<!-- facts: MB-F34 -->
An individual who qualifies as non-domiciled under Cyprus rules is generally outside Special Defence Contribution (SDC) on dividends and interest while eligible. This is not a blanket zero-tax promise: eligibility, other taxes or contributions, and the treatment in the person's circumstances need current specialist review.

The guidance covers dividends and interest, but whether a particular payment and person meet the conditions must be checked against the current rules and the person's filings.

Cyprus has a deemed-domicile test based on being tax resident for 17 of the previous 20 years. Current Tax Department guidance also describes an alternative extension application under the 2026 rules, subject to its conditions. The interaction of these rules is personal and should not be reduced to a guaranteed 17-year benefit.

This treatment does not switch off every other charge, since separate contributions, such as national health system contributions, run under their own rules. Salary, rent, business profit and gains are outside this SDC exemption and follow their own rules.

## Who it applies to, and who it does not
<!-- facts: MB-F34 -->
The regime is built for individuals who newly become Cyprus tax residents. It is not a benefit that applies to everyone living in Cyprus, and it is not automatic on arrival.

Becoming a Cyprus tax resident is its own test. The main route counts the days you spend in Cyprus in a calendar year. There is a second route for people who are not tax resident anywhere else and who have ties to Cyprus such as business activity, employment or an office here. The exact day thresholds and the conditions attached to each route are set in the legislation and are confirmed by a tax advisor for your situation rather than assumed.

Residency alone is still not enough. The non domiciled part is assessed separately, and a person with a Cyprus domicile of origin can be a Cyprus tax resident without being treated as non domiciled.

Leaving has consequences too. If you stop being a Cyprus tax resident, the treatment stops applying for the period in which you are not resident.

## What changes at the end of the window
<!-- facts: MB-F34 -->
The deemed-domicile and SDC rules need to be checked over time; a plan that ignores them is incomplete.

When a person is treated as deemed domiciled or otherwise no longer meets the applicable exemption conditions, SDC may apply under the rules then in force. The Tax Department describes an alternative extension application for 2026, but it has conditions and is not automatic. A tax adviser should review the person's residence history and filings before estimating any change in cost.

There is also a counting question people rarely ask early enough. The window is measured over a defined period of Cyprus tax residency, so years in which you were not a Cyprus tax resident are not treated the same as years in which you were. How the count applies to your own record is confirmed with a tax advisor against the legislation, not estimated from an arrival date.

The practical point is simple. The regime is long, but it is finite and it is personal to you.

## What this does not tell you about your own position
<!-- kind: boundary -->
This describes a regime. It is not an assessment of your tax position and it is not a statement that you qualify.

Whether you become a Cyprus tax resident, whether you are treated as non domiciled, and what the treatment is actually worth to you all depend on facts that are not in this document: your residence history, your other citizenships and residences, where your income arises, and what the country you are leaving will still tax.

Cyprus is also not the only authority involved. The country you are leaving may continue to tax you, and a double tax treaty, where one exists, governs how the two systems interact. That analysis is never a Cyprus only question.

No one can promise a tax outcome. Tax law changes, the facts of a file change, and the treatment is applied by the Tax Department on what is filed and evidenced. A licensed tax advisor assesses your position against the legislation in force, and that is the step that turns this description into an answer for you.
