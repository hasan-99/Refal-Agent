## What the registered address is
<!-- facts: MB-F15 -->
The registered address is an administrative requirement. Every Cyprus company has to have one, and it is the official address of the company in Cyprus where formal correspondence is sent.

In the formation package it is included for four consecutive months, so the company has a compliant address from the moment it exists.

That is the whole of its job. It satisfies the administrative requirement and it gives the authorities a place to write to. It is an entry on the company file, not a place where work happens.

This is also why the registered address is treated as a service of the company rather than as property of the founder. It belongs to the corporate file, not to anyone's day.

## What the registered address is not
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F15 -->
A registered address is not an office, and the two should not be confused.

It is not workspace. Nobody gets a desk, a room, or a key because a company has a registered address.

It is not meeting rooms. If you need somewhere to sit with a client or a bank, that is a separate arrangement.

It is not a mail handling service. Formal correspondence arrives at the registered address, but sorting, scanning and forwarding your post on an ongoing basis is a different service with its own scope.

It is not real commercial presence, which is what is meant by Substance. A company that is actually operated from Cyprus looks different on paper from a company that only has an address there, and the registered address alone does not create that difference.

All of these are available, and all of them are arranged separately from the registered address.

## Why a home address is not the registered address
<!-- facts: MB-F7 -->
This is the question that follows almost every explanation, and the answer is short. The Registered Address included with formation is the company's official address in Cyprus for correspondence. It is not a private office and it is not an apartment, so a home address is not what this service is.

The reasoning behind it is practical. Formal correspondence has to arrive somewhere in Cyprus that stays reachable, and it has to keep arriving when you travel, move, or change tenant. A private flat does all three far more often than a company file should.

So the address is provided as a service of the company, included for four consecutive months from formation, and held with us rather than pointed at your own home. If you genuinely work from a place in Cyprus, that is a separate conversation about real presence, not about where the Registrar sends letters.

## Substance, and why people ask about it
<!-- facts: MB-F15 -->
Substance is the question of whether a company has real commercial presence where it is registered, rather than only a name on a file.

The things people usually mean by it are physical premises, people working in the country, decisions genuinely taken there, and operations that can be seen and evidenced.

It comes up for two reasons. Banks and institutions look at how and where a company actually operates when they assess it. And the way a company is taxed and treated internationally follows where its real activity sits, not only where it is registered.

So the practical reading is this. A registered address answers an administrative requirement. Substance is about real operations, and building it is a separate decision with separate services behind it, from office space through to staffing.

## What we need to know before saying which one you need
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F15 -->
The answer depends on what you are actually planning to do in Cyprus.

Will anyone work from Cyprus, now or later? One person, a small team, or nobody at that stage.

Do you need a place to physically be? Desks, a private room, or meeting rooms you can book when you visit.

How is your post handled? Formal correspondence only, or an ongoing flow that someone needs to sort and forward.

What are you being asked for? A bank, a payment provider, a client or an authority asking for a physical presence changes the requirement, so it helps to know who is asking and what exactly they asked for.

With those answers the choice between an address, an office service and a real presence stops being abstract.
