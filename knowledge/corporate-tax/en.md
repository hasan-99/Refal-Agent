## The corporate tax rate in Cyprus
<!-- facts: MB-F19 -->
The headline corporate income tax rate in Cyprus is 15% for tax years from 2026. The rate alone does not determine a company's tax due; the applicable rules and the company's income and circumstances must be reviewed.

The corporate tax calculation is not simply a percentage of turnover. The taxable amount depends on the applicable tax rules and the company's records. Tax on amounts paid or distributed to an owner is a separate question; its treatment depends on the payment, recipient and applicable rules, including tax residence where relevant.

Income classification, exemptions and special regimes can affect the calculation. A trading, service or share-holding label alone is not enough to determine the company's tax position.

Cyprus was known for a lower headline rate in earlier years. From 2026 the figure to plan with is 15%. If you are reading an older article, an older spreadsheet or an older quote, check its date before you build a budget on it.

## What the rate applies to in practice
<!-- facts: MB-F19 -->
The company's accounting records are needed to assess its taxable position. Revenue less accounting expenses is not, by itself, a complete tax calculation; the applicable tax adjustments and evidence need review.

Accounting periods and filing or payment dates should be confirmed for the company under current Tax Department requirements.

Stopping trade does not by itself settle a company's filing, accounting or tax obligations. Check the company's status, applicable Registrar and Tax Department requirements, and any relevant exceptions with a qualified adviser.

Salary and distributions can receive different treatment. The outcome for the company and recipient depends on the facts and applicable rules, so it needs separate case review.

If more than one country may tax the income, any applicable treaty and domestic rules need to be checked for the countries, income type and circumstances involved.

## What the headline rate does not settle for you
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F23 -->
We state the rate and the mechanics. We do not give a decisive personal tax opinion, and that is deliberate rather than evasive.

What you personally pay depends on your own tax residency, on the treaty between Cyprus and your country, on the type of income, and on how the structure around you is built. None of those inputs are visible from a chat message, and a figure produced without them is a guess with a decimal point on it.

So we do not confirm a number for your situation, and nobody here can promise that you will pay no tax. What we do is take the question to a tax specialist with the real facts of your file, so the answer you act on was built for you rather than for a general case.

## What we need to know to make this concrete
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F19 -->
These details can help a tax specialist identify which parts of your situation need review.

Where are you tax resident today, and do you expect that to change?

What does the company actually do? Trading, services, holding shares or property, or software and intellectual property, because the last one opens a different route.

Where are your customers, and where does the money land?

Do you intend to leave profit inside the company or draw it out, and roughly how soon?

They do not establish your taxable amount or the tax due. A qualified tax specialist must review the records and applicable rules before giving a case-specific estimate.
