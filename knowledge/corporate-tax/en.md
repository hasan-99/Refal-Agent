## The corporate tax rate in Cyprus
<!-- facts: MB-F19 -->
The base corporate tax in Cyprus starts at 15%, and that rate applies from the year 2026.

Two things are worth separating straight away. The 15% is charged on the company's taxable profit, not on its turnover, so it applies to what is left after allowable business expenses rather than to the money coming in. And it is a tax on the company, not on you personally. What you pay once you take money out of the company is a separate layer that depends on your own tax residency.

Cyprus was known for a lower headline rate in earlier years. From 2026 the figure to plan with is 15%. If you are reading an older article, an older spreadsheet or an older quote, check its date before you build a budget on it.

## What the rate applies to in practice
<!-- facts: MB-F19 -->
The company is taxed on profit, so the accounting matters as much as the rate. Revenue minus allowable expenses gives the taxable result, which is why keeping books from the first invoice costs less than reconstructing a whole year at the end of it.

The tax year follows the calendar year, and the filing and payment obligations fall after that year has closed.

A company that has stopped trading is still on the official register, so its reporting and accounting obligations continue. Whether it actually pays corporate tax depends on whether it has taxable profit, not on how busy it was.

Taking profit out of the company sits on top of the 15%. Distributed profit is treated differently from a personal salary, and the result in your hands depends on where you are tax resident.

Cyprus has a wide network of double tax treaties. Whether there is one with your country, and what it says about the specific type of income you have in mind, is read treaty by treaty rather than assumed.

## What the headline rate does not settle for you
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-F23 -->
We state the rate and the mechanics. We do not give a decisive personal tax opinion, and that is deliberate rather than evasive.

What you personally pay depends on your own tax residency, on the treaty between Cyprus and your country, on the type of income, and on how the structure around you is built. None of those inputs are visible from a chat message, and a figure produced without them is a guess with a decimal point on it.

So we do not confirm a number for your situation, and nobody here can promise that you will pay no tax. What we do is take the question to a tax specialist with the real facts of your file, so the answer you act on was built for you rather than for a general case.

## What we need to know to make this concrete
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F19 -->
Four short answers turn the headline rate into something you can plan with.

Where are you tax resident today, and do you expect that to change?

What does the company actually do? Trading, services, holding shares or property, or software and intellectual property, because the last one opens a different route.

Where are your customers, and where does the money land?

Do you intend to leave profit inside the company or draw it out, and roughly how soon?

With those four, the conversation stops being about a rate and starts being about your numbers.
