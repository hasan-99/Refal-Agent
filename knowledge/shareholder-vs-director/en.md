## Who the shareholder is
<!-- facts: MB-F11 -->
The shareholder is the owner. A shareholder holds the shares of the company and the capital behind them, and that holding is what ownership of a Cyprus company actually means.

Ownership is counted in shares, not in job titles. When two people start together, the share split is what records who owns what, and it is agreed before the company is formed rather than assumed afterwards.

A shareholder can be an individual or another company. That matters when a group sits above the Cyprus entity, because the shares are then held by the parent rather than by a person.

Holding shares on its own gives no management role. Management is the second role, and it is separate.

## Who the director is
<!-- facts: MB-F12 -->
The director is the executive, administrative and legal officer who manages the company. The director runs the business day to day: taking the operational decisions, signing on behalf of the company, dealing with the authorities, and keeping the company's filings and obligations in order.

So the shareholder owns and the director manages. The director's authority comes from the office itself, not from holding shares, which is why a company can appoint a director who owns no shares at all.

Because the role carries administrative and legal responsibility, choosing who is appointed is a structural decision rather than a formality.

## The same person can hold both roles
<!-- facts: MB-F13 -->
Put simply, the shareholder is the owner and the director is the person who runs the company, and they can be the same person depending on the structure.

For a single founder that is the usual picture. One person owns the shares and is appointed director, so both roles sit on the same name.

With partners or an international group the roles often split. Several shareholders own, one or two of them manage, and the rest stay purely on the ownership side.

Both pictures are normal. The structure follows how the business is actually run and who needs signing authority.

## What the two role names do not settle
<!-- kind: boundary -->
Knowing the two definitions does not answer everything that follows from them.

It does not set how many directors your company should have, or whether a director needs to be resident in Cyprus. Those follow from the structure you choose and from what your activity, your banking and your tax position require, and they are confirmed file by file.

It does not define a shareholder's exposure to company debts or a director's legal responsibility in a specific situation. Those are legal positions that depend on the facts of the company, not on a general description of the roles.

It does not decide your share split or your tax treatment. Where you are tax resident, and where the other shareholders are resident, both change the picture, and that belongs with a specialist rather than with a definition.

## What we need to know to map the roles onto your file
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F13 -->
Three answers make this concrete.

Who are the owners? One founder, two or more partners, or an existing company that will hold the shares.

Who will actually run the company and sign for it? The same person, one of the partners, or someone appointed from outside.

Is anyone joining later? An investor or a partner expected in a few months changes how the share structure is set up from the start.
