## Qualifying investment
<!-- facts: MB-F30 -->
For eligible third-country nationals applying through the named Cyprus investor immigration permit route, the programme states a qualifying investment of at least €300,000, plus VAT where applicable. Other criteria apply; this figure is not a requirement for every permanent-residence route. Confirm current eligibility and evidence requirements with an immigration specialist.

## Annual income requirement
<!-- facts: MB-F31, MB-F32, MB-F33 -->
Under this investor permit route, the stated annual secure-income requirement is €50,000 for the main applicant, plus €15,000 for a spouse and €10,000 for each dependent minor child. The programme sets source and evidence conditions; in broad terms Category A requires income from abroad, while Categories B, C or D may allow some income from Cyprus activities. Confirm current requirements and supporting proof for the case. These amounts do not apply universally to all residence routes.

## The four investment categories
<!-- facts: MB-F38, MB-F39, MB-F40, MB-F41 -->

- **Category A:** purchase a house or apartment from a development company as a first sale, for at least €300,000 plus VAT.
- **Category B:** other qualifying property types, including offices, shops and hotels, with at least €300,000 invested, subject to the programme conditions.
- **Category C:** under the investor permit route, at least €300,000 invested in the share capital of a Cyprus company, subject to operating-company, physical-presence, at-least-five-employees and other current policy conditions.
- **Category D:** under the investor permit route, at least €300,000 in qualifying Cyprus investment-fund units that meet the programme conditions and are held in Cyprus.

## What these programme facts do not decide
<!-- kind: boundary -->
The source describes a specific investor immigration-permit procedure for eligible third-country nationals, not every permanent-residence route. The investment category is only one part of the criteria: income, evidence and application requirements also apply and depend on the case. These general facts do not establish personal eligibility or an outcome. REFAL must not guarantee approval; it should ask about the intended route and refer case-specific questions for adviser review.
