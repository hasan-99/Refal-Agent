## The conditions the programme sets, in one place
<!-- facts: MB-F30, MB-F31 -->
The conditions for permanent residency in Cyprus come down to three, and a file is assessed against all three together rather than against the headline one.

The first condition is the qualifying investment, held through one of the four recognised categories the programme allows, so the type of asset counts alongside the amount. The second condition is proven annual income arising outside Cyprus, tested for the main applicant and raised for a spouse and for each eligible minor child. The third is documentary: the money used in the transaction has to be traceable, the income has to be evidenced rather than stated, and the compliance file has to stand up to examination.

Those conditions apply as a set. Meeting the investment threshold on its own does not carry a file, and neither does strong income behind an investment that sits outside the four categories.

The conditions for the family follow the same logic. A spouse and eligible minor children are included on the same application, and the income tested rises for each of them rather than staying at the main applicant's level.

## The qualifying investment the programme is built on
<!-- facts: MB-F30 -->
Cyprus grants permanent residency to investors on the basis of one qualifying investment. The minimum is €300,000, plus VAT where VAT is due on what is being acquired.

Three things matter about that figure.

It is a floor set by the programme, not a valuation of any particular asset. It has to be met inside one of four recognised categories, so the type of investment counts as much as the amount. And where VAT is due, the VAT sits on top of the €300,000 rather than inside it, which changes the total cash the file needs.

The investment is one requirement among several. Meeting it does not by itself complete a file, because the income test and the documentation requirements are examined alongside it.

## The annual income the programme tests
<!-- facts: MB-F31, MB-F32, MB-F33 -->
Alongside the investment, the programme tests proven annual income that comes from outside Cyprus.

- Main applicant: €50,000 per year.
- Spouse: an additional €15,000 per year.
- Each eligible minor child: an additional €10,000 per year.

So a couple with two eligible minor children is tested against €50,000 plus €15,000 plus €10,000 plus €10,000.

Two points are often missed. The income has to arise outside Cyprus, which is why a salary from a Cyprus employer is treated differently from foreign dividends, rent or a pension. And it has to be proven with documents rather than stated on a form, so the evidence trail matters as much as the number.

## Category A: new residential property
<!-- facts: MB-F38 -->
Category A is the most prominent option for customers, and most Cyprus files are built on it.

The investment is a new residential property, a house or an apartment, at a minimum of €300,000 plus VAT where it applies. The defining condition is that it must be a first sale, bought directly from the developer. A resale between private owners does not qualify under this category, however good the property is.

That one condition shapes the whole search. It narrows the market to developer stock, it ties the file to the developer's contract and payment plan, and it makes the legal checks on the developer and on the title a core part of the work rather than a formality.

Which units are available, and on what terms, is confirmed live from the property database at the time you are looking, because that information moves.

## Category B: commercial and other property types
<!-- facts: MB-F39 -->
Category B covers property that is not new residential. Offices, shops and hotels sit here, at the same minimum of €300,000.

The emphasis in this category is on combined or redeveloped commercial property, so a file is often built from more than one unit, or from a building that has been reconstructed for a new commercial use.

In practice Category B behaves differently from Category A. The return profile, the tenant risk and the legal and technical due diligence are commercial rather than residential, and the resale market is narrower. It tends to suit someone who already has commercial property experience and wants the investment and the residency file to do the same job.

## Category C: share capital in a Cyprus company
<!-- facts: MB-F40 -->
Category C is an investment of at least €300,000 in the share capital of a Cyprus company.

The qualifying condition here is substance. The company has to genuinely operate, employ people and have a real presence in Cyprus. A shell incorporated only to absorb the amount does not satisfy this category, and the file is examined on what the company actually does.

That makes Category C the most operationally demanding of the four. It brings payroll, accounting, audit and continuing corporate obligations with it, and the activity has to keep standing up over time rather than only on the day the file is submitted. It fits an investor who wanted a working business in Cyprus anyway, not someone looking for the lightest route.

## Category D: units in Cyprus investment funds
<!-- facts: MB-F41 -->
Category D is an investment of at least €300,000 in units of qualifying Cyprus investment funds, specifically AIF and AIFLNP vehicles.

It is the lightest of the four to administer. There is no property to manage and no company to run, so the ongoing obligations are smaller. What replaces them is fund selection, because the fund has to be of a qualifying type and the units have to be held in the applicant's own name.

The trade off is control and liquidity. A fund runs on its own terms, its own risk and its own redemption rules, and the value of units moves in both directions. Fund performance is never part of what we describe, and any projection you are shown belongs to the fund manager's own documentation.

## Documents, income evidence and the compliance side
<!-- facts: MB-F30, MB-F31 -->
A residency file is a document file as much as an investment.

The investment has to be traceable. Banks and the immigration authorities expect to see where the money used in this specific transaction came from, and in some cases the wider history of how the wealth was built. It is a standard compliance step applied to every file, not a judgement about you.

The income is treated the same way. €50,000 written on a form is not the test. The test is €50,000 that can be evidenced with documents from outside Cyprus which an examiner can verify, and the same applies to the addition for a spouse and the addition for each eligible minor child.

The exact document list depends on your nationality, where your income arises and which of the four categories you use, so it is set per file rather than from a generic checklist.

## What this description does not decide
<!-- kind: boundary -->
Eligibility is decided by the Cyprus authorities on the complete file, not by anyone describing the programme. Everything above is the criteria the programme sets. It is not an assessment of your case and not a prediction of your outcome.

Plainly: no one can guarantee an approval, and we do not. The authorities examine the investment, the income evidence, the compliance documentation and the personal circumstances of every applicant, and they can ask for more at any point.

There is no timeline we can promise either. Processing time belongs to the authorities and to third parties such as banks and the issuers of your documents.

Permanent residency is also not citizenship. It is a residence status and does not in itself give a Cyprus passport. Naturalisation is a separate process with its own law and its own conditions.

Residence conditions, what a previous refusal means for a new application, and how the rules apply to your nationality are matters for a licensed immigration specialist to assess against the legislation in force when you apply.
