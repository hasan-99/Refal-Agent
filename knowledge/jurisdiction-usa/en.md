## What a US company is good at
<!-- facts: MB-J4 -->
A US LLC or Inc is an excellent vehicle for e-commerce and for selling through Stripe and the wider US payment ecosystem. If the business is an online store, a subscription product or an app charging cards, that route is well trodden and the tooling around it is mature. We say that plainly, because it is true and a lot of founders start there for exactly that reason.

Two things sit on the other side of it.

Tax is complex for non residents. There are federal and state layers, filing obligations that exist whether or not the company made a profit, and treatment that shifts with the owner's own residence and with where the work is actually performed.

And a US entity gives no direct access to the European Union market. EU VAT registration, EU invoicing and what European customers and suppliers expect from a counterparty are not solved by incorporating in the United States.

## What the Cyprus side adds
<!-- facts: MB-J4 -->
A Cyprus company is not automatically a replacement for a US one. In some structures the two sit side by side, each doing the part it is good at.

**EU market access.** A Cyprus company is an EU company: EU VAT registration where the activity requires one, EU invoicing, and a European counterparty for European customers and suppliers.

**Real Substance.** Offices, staff and management on the ground. That is what a bank, a payment provider or a tax authority looks for when it asks where the company is actually run from.

**Residency and property alongside the business.** One decision can cover the company, where the family lives and a tangible asset, instead of three separate projects in three places.

**Proximity.** For a founder in our region, the time zone, the travel and the business culture are closer than a US operating base.

Which of those matters depends entirely on who the customers are and on whether anyone intends to move.

## What this comparison does not decide
<!-- kind: boundary; facts: MB-J0 -->
We do not run the United States down and we do not claim Cyprus is the right answer for everyone. For a US facing e-commerce business a US entity can be the cleaner structure.

The right jurisdiction depends on the specific business. Without knowing where your customers are, how you take payment, what the activity is, and whether residency and property matter, a comparison is not a real comparison.

Several questions here are outside what we can answer. The choice between US states, federal and state filing positions, and US immigration routes belong to a US adviser. Whether a bank or a payment provider accepts a given company is decided by that institution, in the United States and in Cyprus alike. And whether selling to American customers creates a US taxable presence is a question for a tax specialist in both countries, not something a general rule settles.

## What we need to know to make it concrete
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-J4 -->
Four answers move this from countries to your file.

Where are the customers? European, American, regional, or spread across more than one of those.

How is the money taken? Card payments through a gateway, bank transfers, marketplaces, or invoices to other businesses.

Do residency and property matter to you or to the family, now or later?

How close does the operating environment need to be? Working from a European time zone near our region is a different daily reality from running everything on US hours.

With those, the tax questions go to a tax specialist in each country involved, and the structure discussion becomes about your operation rather than about two flags.
