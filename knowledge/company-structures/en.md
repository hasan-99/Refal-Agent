## A new Ltd is not the automatic answer
<!-- facts: MB-F10 -->
When someone asks how to open a company in Cyprus, the quick answer is a new Cyprus Ltd. That answer is right for many people and wrong for some of the most important cases.

If you represent a large commercial entity or an already existing international company, there are three distinct routes, not one.

- **A new Cyprus Ltd.** A standalone Cyprus company with its own shareholders, incorporated from scratch.
- **A Branch of the foreign company.** An extension of the company that already exists, established in Cyprus rather than created as a separate new entity.
- **A Subsidiary.** A Cyprus company owned by the existing foreign parent, so the parent stays the owner and the Cyprus entity is its own company.

Which of the three applies depends on whether an existing international entity is involved at all, and on what that entity already does. Choosing before that is known is guesswork, which is why the question gets asked first rather than last.

## Why the distinction shows up before anything is filed
<!-- kind: example; facts: MB-F10 -->
Two enquiries that read almost identically go in different directions.

A founder with no company anywhere, starting a trading or services business. A new Cyprus Ltd is the normal starting point, and the conversation is about the activity, the shareholders and the name.

A director of an established international company that wants an EU presence. Here a Branch or a Subsidiary is a genuine option, and treating the enquiry as a simple new Ltd registration skips the decision that actually matters: how the Cyprus presence relates to the parent, who owns what, and how the group is meant to look afterwards.

The second case is a corporate structuring review, not a registration form. It is routed to the corporate advisory team, because the cost of getting it wrong is not the registration fee, it is the restructuring later.

## Being the sole owner, or bringing partners in
<!-- kind: boundary -->
Two ownership questions arrive with almost every enquiry. Can the company be owned by one person, and how many partners are needed before a company can be opened at all.

Neither is answered here as a general rule. Shareholder and director minimums, and the share capital that sits with them, are confirmed by the corporate team against the requirements in force for the route you take, because a new Cyprus Ltd, a Branch and a Subsidiary are not examined on the same terms.

What the route does decide is the shape of the ownership. A new Cyprus Ltd is owned by its own shareholders, whoever those turn out to be. A Subsidiary is owned by the existing foreign parent, so the owner is a company rather than a person. A Branch has no separate ownership at all, because it is an extension of the company that already exists rather than a new one.

So the useful first answer is which of the three routes is on the table. Who can be the sole owner, and who else can be brought in later, follows from that, and the corporate team confirms the detail against your case.

## What a general description cannot decide for you
<!-- kind: boundary -->
This page explains that three routes exist. It does not pick one for you.

It does not say which route suits your group. That depends on the existing entity, where it is registered and tax resident, what the activity is, and what the owners want the structure to look like in a few years.

It does not state share capital levels, shareholder or director minimums, residency conditions, or the mechanics of moving from one structure to another as general facts. Those follow from the route chosen and from current requirements, so they are confirmed by the corporate team against your case rather than quoted generically.

It does not promise a particular treatment by a bank, a tax authority or a registry. Those decisions belong to the institution concerned.

Holding versus operating is a separate question from Branch versus Subsidiary versus new Ltd. The two are often mixed together, and they are decided on different grounds.

## What we need to know to answer it properly
<!-- kind: discovery; facts: MB-F10 -->
Four answers settle which of the three routes is even on the table.

Does a company already exist outside Cyprus? If yes, where is it registered and what does it actually do.

What is Cyprus meant to be? The operating arm, the owner of assets or shares, or an extension of what already runs elsewhere.

Who owns it? The shareholders, whether they are individuals or companies, and where they are resident.

What is the plan after this one? New markets, an investor joining, or a group that will have more than one entity.

With those four, the answer stops being a list of three options and becomes one recommended route with the reasons attached.
