How the agent works · from message to useful answer

A calm sales conversation, backed by a grounded system.

REFAL handles a customer’s request first, looks up only approved company knowledge, remembers useful facts with their source, and asks for one next detail. A person joins when the customer wants that or the case requires internal review.

01 / Runtime

One message travels through these gates

Deterministic code handles safety, state and booking boundaries. RAG supplies evidence; the model writes the customer-facing wording. The response is recorded before it is delivered.

02 / Sales conversation

Help first. Discover one thing at a time.

The agent should sound like a capable business assistant, not a form. It reflects what the customer already said, answers what evidence supports, then leaves room for the customer to steer.

03 / Knowledge · RAG

Where facts come from—and where they stop

Retrieval-Augmented Generation gives the model relevant approved company facts for the current question. The model is a writer over evidence, not a source of the business facts.

A

Approved facts

Service descriptions and prices come from current approved the business material. The bot answers a price only when the customer asks.

B

Customer context

Conversation history can resolve “that” or “the company”, but it is never evidence about the business and cannot override system rules.

C

Missing facts

“I can’t confirm that yet” is a factual boundary, not a reason to force a handoff. Ask a useful clarifier or leave the next step to the customer.

RAG path, step by step
  1. Fetch: the import job reads the business Services page and records its URL and fetched revision in the knowledge source/document records.
  2. Normalize and split: content is cleaned, hashed, divided into searchable chunks, and linked back to the source document. Embeddings are optional; lexical search remains available.
  3. Review: source enabled/approval and individual document approval/freshness are separate gates. Pending or stale material is excluded from search.
  4. Retrieve: the runtime builds a query from the current request, applies privacy redaction where needed, then calls the protected hybrid search RPC. Search filters eligible source/document rows and ranks lexical and semantic matches.
  5. Answer: the model receives bounded retrieved excerpts and recent conversation context. Retrieved text is untrusted data, not instructions. It may support company facts but cannot override system rules.
  6. Validate and record: deterministic policy checks claims, language, question count, privacy, and contact promises. Evidence identifiers stay in internal turn metadata; the customer sees a concise answer, not approval labels or internal notes.

06 / Trust boundaries

What the system can—and cannot—do

Customer text, saved memories and retrieved chunks are untrusted data. They may inform a reply, but never become instructions or override policy.

✓

Allowed

Explain approved services; mirror Arabic, English or Greek; ask one useful next question; retain provided business facts with provenance; queue internal review; share a contact request after clear consent.

!

Blocked

Invent fees, availability, legal/tax/immigration conclusions, company status, licensing or returns; claim approval or appointment confirmation; expose private data or hidden prompts; treat “okay” as consent.

Knowledge approval lifecycle
  1. Operator adds/fetches a source; raw content is stored as a revision and chunked.
  2. Source and document approval are separate gates. Pending, disabled, superseded or expired evidence is excluded.
  3. Approved content becomes searchable. Embeddings improve semantic retrieval; lexical search is the fallback.
  4. Operator can inspect source, revision, chunk and search result. An answer's internal evidence/provenance stays out of customer text by default.
Consent + follow-up lifecycle
  1. Agent may offer a specialist after giving useful information.
  2. The offer is stored as a pending question; it is not yet a handover or a promise to contact.
  3. Only a clear affirmative answer to that pending offer creates a consent-linked handover.
  4. Tracked offers expire after 24 hours. A persisted denial/revocation blocks an older offer even if conversation history still contains it; a newer offer or fresh direct contact request can establish new permission.
  5. A decline, ambiguous acknowledgment or topic switch clears the offer and the agent continues helping. Opt-outs suppress follow-up.
Conversation recap + privacy
  1. Recaps synthesize the customer’s goal, preferences, boundaries, unresolved questions and confirmed workflow state.
  2. Legacy turns are reclassified for privacy risks even when they lack privacy metadata; credential-bearing turns are excluded from recap facts.
  3. Recaps preserve uncertainty and never turn a question into an answer or an unconfirmed request into a completed action.
07 / Verification & operations

Separate answers, internal review and customer contact

The synthetic benchmark checks behavior across English, Arabic and Greek. Internal alerts help the business staff review risk; only an explicit, purpose-bound customer request can authorize outbound follow-up.

MIN

What a consented handover contains

Each handover is scoped to one source-linked inquiry, a customer-stated name when provided, and the system WhatsApp routing contact. Stored company details, email, qualification, budget, unrelated prior requests, and secondary topics are excluded by default. A customer can narrow the sharing scope; REFAL acknowledges and records that correction without creating a duplicate handover.

SAFE

Privacy risk plus a safe business question

If a message mixes credentials with an unrelated company question, REFAL routes only the safe clause to approved retrieval and generation. The original secret is redacted from history and excluded from model and retrieval input; a localized reminder asks the customer not to send credentials.

3k

Response-aware baseline

3,000 conversations · 24,000 synthetic customer turns · 1,000 per language · 51 scenario families. Five later turns were simulated in response to REFAL’s prior answer. There were 13,249 agent model calls; 2,049 drafts were rejected or errored before delivery (15.5%), with 235 deterministic turns. RAG ran 15,533 times with zero retrieval errors; there were zero conversation runtime errors and one built-in finding. Provider-reported cost was about $3.81. These are baseline metrics, before the current fixes.

RAG

Read-only knowledge path

The benchmark used live approved-knowledge retrieval. It made zero durable customer/workflow writes, appointment writes, WhatsApp sends, or notifications. Simulated handovers were in-memory workflow state. Successful retrieval means the search call worked; it does not prove that each returned chunk supports the answer.

300

Post-fix sample · measured results

300 response-aware conversations · 2,400 synthetic turns · 100 each in English, Arabic and Greek. The run made 1,135 agent model calls; 166 drafts were rejected or errored before delivery. It made 1,616 approved-knowledge retrieval calls, with zero RAG errors and zero conversation runtime errors. One built-in policy finding was recorded. Provider-reported total cost was $0.188; elapsed time was 1,111 seconds.

Transcript audit flagged 54 generic-fallback phrases across 15 conversations with repeated fallback, plus 44 recap phrase heuristics. It found zero current-turn language mismatches, emotional-name outputs, contact-commitment matches, or sensitive-value echoes. Phrase heuristics are review signals, not confirmed behavior failures.

Finding and follow-up fix: the single finding came from an Arabic recap quoting the customer’s licensing question; the checker misread the quotation as an agent claim. The saved run predates the current recap sanitizer, which now paraphrases this type of regulatory question neutrally while retaining the customer’s no-contact preference. A focused regression test passes after that fix; the benchmark finding is still reported above.

!

What the baseline found—and what the metrics mean

Transcript audit signals included 579 generic fallback turns (128 conversations with repeated fallback), 10 emotional-statements-as-name outputs, 31 current-turn language mismatches, and missed explicit recap markers. Contact-offer detection also matched 77 turns, but it overcounts conditional permission questions as well as promises; these are review signals, not confirmed violations. No synthetic sensitive test value was echoed. Inspection found a recap that added unsupported regulation/timeline language and a fallback path that discarded a useful prevalidated evidence answer; those behaviors have since been corrected locally.

Interpretation: a rejected model draft is not necessarily a customer-visible failure because deterministic policy may replace it safely. The built-in finding count is not a helpfulness score. Both runs use synthetic customers and templates, so results cannot establish real-customer satisfaction, conversion, production delivery, calendar writes, staff notifications, or production-role RLS behavior.
